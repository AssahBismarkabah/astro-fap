You read that correctly. It still feels strange to call it a company, but it is one. We started Aveling Research, an independent quantitative research firm based in Douala, Cameroon.

Quantitative finance is a broad and difficult field. Large firms in the United States, Europe, and other parts of the world compete on speed, colocation, information, data, capital, and the engineering talent required to turn those resources into models. Competing with them directly on those terms would make little sense. They have spent decades building that machinery.

We arrived at a different direction after doing a series of systematic analyses. African economies and markets produce a large amount of financial, economic, environmental, and operational data, but much of it is slow, fragmented, and difficult to extract. It doesn't naturally arrive as a clean feed that can be loaded into a model. That makes it easy to overlook.

Aveling Research is being built around that gap. The aim is to bring together the engineering and quantitative talent required to collect this data, structure it, and test whether it contains useful relationships. The advantage we're looking for isn't faster execution. It's the ability to study information that other market participants may not have processed well enough to use.

The company will initially be research-heavy, as any quantitative firm should be. We will use quantitative analysis, software engineering, algorithms, AI, and machine learning to build datasets, classify information, investigate market relationships, and automate the research workflow. The methods matter because most apparent relationships disappear when they are tested against new data, realistic costs, and changing market conditions.

The work can produce value in more than one form. A useful dataset or model may become research for another firm. A relationship that survives validation may become one of our own systematic trading strategies. A failed relationship still becomes a recorded result that prevents us from spending time and capital on the same idea again.

Our initial focus is Africa, but the company isn't named after one geography or one strategy. The research may eventually extend into other markets where our tools and methods provide an advantage. Competing in the large global markets remains possible, but it isn't the starting point.

For now, the work is direct: collect difficult African data, make it usable, test whether it predicts anything, and keep only what survives.
